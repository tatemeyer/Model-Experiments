# Literature registry

Every paper this project has evaluated or drawn on, deduped from citations
scattered across `experiments/`. Check here before re-proposing a paper as
a "new" lead — if it's already **Tried**, the verdict and notes explain
why it did or didn't transfer, so a future issue can build on that instead
of re-deriving it. Add a row (and update verdict) whenever an issue cites
or tests a new paper — see `experiments/TEMPLATE.md`.

| Wang, Sankaran, Perdikaris, "Respecting Causality is All You Need for Training Physics-Informed Neural Networks" | arXiv:2203.07404 | `long-horizon-collapse/023-long-horizon-causal.md` | ❌ Tried — didn't help | Causal loss-reweighting, faithfully implemented, statistically indistinguishable from uniform weighting at every `epsilon` tried (four orders of magnitude). Diagnosed why: its gating signal (cumulative earlier-chunk residual) targets *unconverged-residual lag*, but this project's collapse is a *degenerate low-residual* pathology the same formula can't detect. |
| Krishnapriyan et al., "Characterizing possible failure modes in physics-informed neural networks," NeurIPS 2021 | arXiv:2109.01050 | `long-horizon-collapse/032-curriculum-long-horizon.md` | ⚠️ Tried — partial win | Short-to-long training curriculum. Real, consistent improvement (0.899-0.914 vs. uniform's 0.923-0.926) — the only fix in the long-horizon thread that moved the number at all — but modest, and the same collapse mechanism reappears partway through the widened domain. Not a qualitative fix. |
| Wang, Teng, Perdikaris, "Understanding and Mitigating Gradient Flow Pathologies in Physics-Informed Neural Networks," SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 2021 | arXiv:2001.04536 | Flagged as unexplored in `020`, `two-mode-spectral-bias/025-num-bands-sweep.md`, `long-horizon-collapse/030-pseudo-sequence-long-horizon.md`; tried in `long-horizon-collapse/034-ntk-reweighted-long-horizon.md` | 🔻 Tried — actively worse | NTK-based adaptive loss-term reweighting. The single most-repeated unexplored lead across three prior issues, but a faithful implementation *regresses* below doing nothing (1.000-1.134 vs. uniform's 0.923-0.926). Diagnosed mechanistically: the scheme assumes the PDE-residual term dominates and needs down-weighting; this project's collapse inverts that (PDE residual is the *easy* term), so the formula crushes the IC/BC weights instead of correcting anything — never re-tried against the two-mode target, where the assumption might hold. |
| Leiteritz & Pflueger, "How to Avoid Trivial Solutions in Physics-Informed Neural Networks" | arXiv:2112.05620 | `long-horizon-collapse/035-antitrivial-regularizer.md` | 🔻 Tried — worse than uniform | The literal target of the long-horizon thread's most-flagged remaining lead: an explicit penalty (max squared gradient of the raw PDE residual across collocation points, eq. 8/13) meant to obstruct the trivial solution directly rather than reweight around it. Faithfully implemented (generalized from the paper's 1D-input eq. 13 to this project's 2D `(x,t)` domain via the residual gradient's combined `x`/`t` magnitude) — lands at 0.968-0.972 across seeds 0/1/2/7, worse than uniform (0.923-0.926) and causal (0.923-0.925), though tighter across seeds and not as bad as pseudo-sequence (0.979-1.102) or NTK reweighting (1.000-1.134). Diagnosed via per-chunk instrumentation: the penalty's own value is *highest* where the model still tracks real oscillation and decreases *monotonically* to near-zero deep in the collapsed plateau -- the paper's mechanism assumes a trivial solution creates a localized residual-gradient spike (from an abrupt truth-to-trivial switch in a collocation-starved domain), but this project's collapse is a smooth, domain-wide settling where the trivial region is the *cheapest* place to satisfy the penalty, not a place it flags. Paying the penalty's nonzero cost elsewhere pulls fitting capacity away from the IC term instead, mildly reproducing (in miniature) the same trade-off that badly hurt NTK reweighting. |
| Bizzi et al., "Neuro-Spectral Architectures for Causal Physics-Informed Networks," NeurIPS 2025 | arXiv:2509.04966 | `long-horizon-collapse/036-neusa-long-horizon.md` | ✅ Tried — worked | NeuSA: project onto the Dirichlet sine basis (exact BC), reduce the wave equation to a finite-dimensional ODE for spectral coefficients (exact IC via quadrature), integrate with a small learned correction via RK4. The first fix in this thread that's a genuinely different architecture, not a loss/schedule change on the plain coordinate MLP — essentially solves the long-horizon collapse (relative L2 0.00225-0.00230 across seeds 0/1/2/7 vs. every prior fix's ~0.90-1.13). Confirms the paper's causal-by-construction claim for this project's own wave-equation target. |
| Xu, Liu, Nassereldine, Xiong, "FP64 is All You Need: Rethinking Failure Modes in Physics-Informed Neural Networks," NeurIPS 2025 (University at Buffalo, SUNY) | arXiv:2505.10949 | `num-bands-gap/038-fp64-precision.md` | ❌ Tried — didn't help | Claims FP32 causes L-BFGS's convergence test (`tolerance_change`) to fire prematurely inside a "failure phase" that's actually the same loss basin as the true solution, misattributing this to genuine local optima. Tested directly against this project's own `num_bands=4` L-BFGS plateau (issues #6/#8), at the *same* iteration budget as the documented FP32 numbers: FP64 leaves the original 32-hidden/200-point plateau unchanged (0.889-0.922 vs. FP32's 0.822-0.851) and doesn't meaningfully change the already-fixed 64-hidden config either (0.028-0.058 vs. FP32's 0.018-0.041) — not a precision artifact within this project's controlled comparison. Partial mechanistic corroboration at a different level: FP64 does stop L-BFGS's convergence test from exiting early (confirmed via a reduced-budget ablation that collapsed the 64-hidden result to total failure), consistent with the paper's own account of *why* FP64 costs more per training run (~10-60x wall time here, vs. the paper's own reported 1.1-1.3x on an H100 GPU — CPU vs. GPU FP64 throughput plausibly explains most of that gap) — but that extra genuine computation doesn't cross into the paper's "success phase" within the budget tested here. Whether a much larger budget eventually would is untested and would abandon this project's budget-matched comparison. |

**Legend:** ✅ tried and worked · ⚠️ tried, partial/modest win · ❌ tried, no effect · 🔻 tried, actively worse than doing nothing · 📖 cited as theory/motivation, not itself a tested fix.
